===== Story / Fairy Tale =====

Mira's librarian keeps a catalogue of the kingdom's small collections. A larger archive can hold that entire catalogue as one entry. The librarian refuses to make a single catalogue that includes absolutely every collection, including every catalogue constructed from it: the rule “list exactly the catalogues that do not list themselves” would ask for a catalogue that lists itself exactly when it does not.

The solution is to say which archive a discussion uses. Moving to a larger archive is allowed when its existence has been assumed; it is not a magic final archive containing everything.

**Story key:** archives suggest universe levels and catalogues suggest collections. **Where the analogy stops:** a set-universe is not a finite storage room or a practical memory limit. Its closure properties and existence assumptions are mathematical, and the membership relation is not literal shelving.

===== Plain Language =====

“The category of all small sets” needs a clear meaning for “small.” The collection of all sets cannot itself be a set in ordinary set theory: Russell's construction produces a contradiction. Universes provide a controlled context for saying which sets are allowed and which collections belong to a larger context.

Small, large and huge are relative labels, not measurements of how many elements we can write down. An infinite set can be small. A category may have small hom sets but too many objects to be small; this is why “locally small” will be a separate condition.

===== Mathematics =====

==== Definition 1.1 ====

The primary calls a set \(U\) a set-universe when it contains \(\mathbb N\), is closed under taking subsets of its elements, is closed under power sets, and satisfies indexed-union closure: if \(I\in U\) and \(A_i\in U\) for every \(i\in I\), then \(\bigcup_{i\in I}A_i\in U\). These are the source's stated closure requirements; do not silently replace them by a different textbook definition of a universe.

The union condition requires the index set itself to belong to \(U\). It does not assert closure under an arbitrary external collection of members of \(U\).

==== Remark 1.2 ====

Suppose \(U\in U\). Form \(R=\{A\in U:A\notin A\}\). Since \(R\subseteq U\), subset closure gives \(R\in U\). The defining property of \(R\), applied to this member, then gives \(R\in R\iff R\notin R\), a contradiction. Thus \(U\notin U\). The same pattern explains the difficulty with a putative set of all sets.

==== Convention 1.3 ====

The notes assume three levels:

$$U_{\mathrm{small}}\subseteq U_{\mathrm{large}}\subseteq U_{\mathrm{huge}},\qquad U_{\mathrm{small}}\in U_{\mathrm{large}},\quad U_{\mathrm{large}}\in U_{\mathrm{huge}}.$$

Their existence is an assumption of the treatment, not a consequence proved in the preceding paragraph. Inclusion and membership are distinct assertions and both are recorded.

==== Definition 1.4 ====

A small set is a member of \(U_{\mathrm{small}}\); a large set is a member of \(U_{\mathrm{large}}\); a huge set is a member of \(U_{\mathrm{huge}}\). The word “set” without a modifier means small in the primary's convention. Since the universes are nested, these labels are not mutually exclusive: every small set is large. The small universe itself is large by assumption but is not small by the preceding argument.

**Example and boundary.** \(\mathbb N\) is small, and its power set is small by the stated closure axiom. That does not make the collection of all small sets small. “All” must always be read relative to a specified universe.
