====== Adjunctions: a natural correspondence of maps ======

===== Story / Fairy Tale =====

Mira's workshop accepts a list of symbols and builds all finite formal combinations of them. The royal accountant can either specify a rule for every completed combination or simply choose where each original symbol goes. The workshop's rules extend the second choice uniquely to the first.

There are two ways to describe the same instruction, and changing the list or the receiving workshop respects the translation. The workshop and accountant have an adjunction. They have not become interchangeable: forgetting a workshop's structure and freely rebuilding it can produce a different object.

**Story key:** freely building is a left adjoint, forgetting is a right adjoint, and extension/restriction is the hom correspondence. **Where the analogy stops:** not every adjunction is a free/forgetful construction; “best” is not a definition. Units and counits are not generally isomorphisms.

===== Plain Language =====

An adjunction matches maps of two different shapes. A map from the freely generated vector space on \(S\) to \(V\) is determined by the image of each basis label. Thus linear maps \(k^{(S)}\to V\) correspond to functions \(S\to U(V)\). This is a bijection of maps, not a claim that the objects \(k^{(S)}\) and \(S\) are equal.

Naturality means that reindexing the input or mapping the output commutes with the correspondence. Choosing an unrelated bijection of hom sets for every pair of objects would not establish an adjunction.

===== Mathematics =====

**Definition 3.1.** An adjunction \(L\dashv R\), with \(L:C\to D\), \(R:D\to C\), includes natural transformations \(\eta:1_C\Rightarrow RL\) and \(\varepsilon:LR\Rightarrow1_D\) satisfying

$$\varepsilon_{Lx}\circ L(\eta_x)=1_{Lx},\qquad R(\varepsilon_y)\circ\eta_{Ry}=1_{Ry}.$$

The first composite is \(Lx\to LRLx\to Lx\) in \(D\); the second is \(Ry\to RLRy\to Ry\) in \(C\). These triangle identities express that translating a map there and back returns it unchanged.

**Example 3.2: all four adjunctions.**

  * \((-)_{+}\dashv U:\mathbf{Set}_*\to\mathbf{Set}\): a pointed map \(S\sqcup\{*\}\to(X,x_0)\) is uniquely specified by its values on \(S\); the extra point must go to \(x_0\). The unit includes \(S\) into its enlargement. The counit sends the newly added point to the existing basepoint and keeps the original elements.
  * \(k^{(-)}\dashv U:\mathbf{Vect}_k\to\mathbf{Set}\): a function \(S\to U(V)\) extends uniquely by finite linear combinations. The unit sends \(s\) to \(e_s\); the counit evaluates a formal linear combination of vectors in \(V\).
  * \(D\dashv U:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}\): every function from a discrete space is continuous, so \(\mathbf{Top}(D(S),X)\cong\mathbf{Set}(S,U(X))\).
  * The adjunction \(U\dashv I\), with \(U:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}\) and \(I:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Top}\): every function into an indiscrete space is continuous, so \(\mathbf{Set}(U(X),S)\cong\mathbf{Top}(X,I(S))\).

Thus the topology examples form \(D\dashv U\dashv I\). For the last two, the hom correspondences leave the underlying functions unchanged, so naturality follows from function composition. In the first two, restriction/extension commutes with reindexing generators and postcomposing structure-preserving maps. These explanations verify why the displayed pairs are adjunctions.

**Theorem 3.3.** An adjunction structure on \((L,R)\) is equivalently a family of bijections

$$\Phi_{x,y}:D(Lx,y)\cong C(x,Ry)$$

natural contravariantly in \(x\) and covariantly in \(y\). Hom collections are taken in an adequate universe; smallness of all of \(C,D\) is not being asserted.

The source gives the functions \(g\mapsto R(g)\eta_x\) and \(f\mapsto\varepsilon_yL(f)\). In the converse direction it specifies \(\eta_x=\Phi_{x,Lx}(1_{Lx})\) and \(\varepsilon_y=\Phi^{-1}_{Ry,y}(1_{Ry})\).

**Two embedded exercise assignments (p. 12) ask for the mutual inverse and naturality checks, and for naturality and triangle identities of the constructed unit and counit. Those proofs and their Lean solutions are intentionally withheld.** The formulas above are source-supplied data, not solutions of those assignments.

Riehl's Definition 4.1.1 starts from the hom-bijection formulation. The primary starts from unit and counit. This difference is a choice of presentation, resolved by the theorem, rather than two incompatible meanings of adjunction.
