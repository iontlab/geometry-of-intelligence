==== 3.2. Equivalences of strict categories ====

Recall that a functor \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\) between strict categories is an isomorphism in \(\mathcal{CAT}_s\) if there is a functor \(G:\mathcal D\to\mathcal C\) such that \(GF=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal C}\) and \(FG=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal D}\).

Since we have a notion of morphisms between functors, namely the natural transformations, we can weaken the notion of sameness between strict categories as follows.

**Definition 3.4.** A functor \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\) between strict categories is an **equivalence** if there is a functor \(G:\mathcal D\to\mathcal C\) and natural isomorphisms \(\eta:\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal C}\overset{\cong}{\Longrightarrow}GF\) and \(\varepsilon:FG\overset{\cong}{\Longrightarrow}\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal D}\).

//Example 3.5.// The following classes of functors give special cases of equivalences.

  * Any isomorphism in \(\mathcal{CAT}_s\) is an equivalence of strict categories.
  * Given an adjunction \(L\dashv R\) such that the unit and the counit are natural isomorphisms, then both \(L\) and \(R\) are equivalences of strict categories; we refer to this as an **adjoint equivalence**.

Fact: every equivalence of strict categories can be promoted to an adjoint equivalence; see [Rie16, Corollary 4.3.6]. We won’t use this fact in this course.

**Definition 3.6.** A functor \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\) between strict categories is **essentially surjective** if, for all objects \(d\in\mathcal D\), there is an object \(c\in\mathcal C\) and an isomorphism \(\varphi:Fc\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}d\) in \(\mathcal D\).

**Theorem 3.7** (Fundamental Theorem)**.** //A functor \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\) between strict categories is an equivalence if and only if it is essentially surjective on objects and fully faithful.//

//Proof.// Next time. \(\square\)

**Construction 3.8.** The **walking isomorphism** is the strict category \(\mathcal I:=\{0\cong1\}\) with two objects \(0\) and \(1\), whose only morphisms are the identities and mutually inverse isomorphisms \(f:0\to1\) and \(f^{-1}:1\to0\). Observe that the hom sets of \(\mathcal I\) are all singletons.

**Proposition 3.9.** //The functor \(0:[0]\to\mathcal I\) is an equivalence of strict categories.//

//Proof.// It is surjective on objects up to isomorphisms, since \(0\cong0\) in \(\mathcal I\), and it is fully faithful since, for the unique object \(*\in[0]\), we have a bijection

$$[0](*,*)=\{\operatorname{id}_*\}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\{\operatorname{id}_0\}=\mathcal I(0,0).\quad\square$$
